In biology, the cycle is simple: you consume, you digest, and you excrete. If any part of that system fails, you get sick.

I’ve realized the same applies to our digital lives.

For the past several months, I have been consuming at a breakneck speed. My “diet” has been a relentless stream of LinkedIn insights, Medium deep-dives, X threads, and niche blogs. But without a way to process it, all that consumption resulted in a kind of “intellectual heartburn”—discomfort, confusion, and a cluttered mind.

The Digestion Phase

To settle the heartburn, I started building. Digestion, for me, is the act of turning passive consumption into active creation. I moved from being a spectator to an architect, building projects ranging from the sentimental to the ambitious:

  • A Bengali Calendar app for my mother.
  • A custom MCP to manage my social media presence.
  • Reviving my incomplete book manuscripts.
  • An AI Agent specifically for Python coding.
  • A “Deep Research & Fake Filter” to clean up my daily information intake.

This is on top of my day-to-day at Altera, where I live in GitHub Copilot. For my personal projects, I’ve moved from Claude Code to experimenting with Anti Gravity.

Building has helped. The confusion is clearing. But it has created a new problem: I am glued to my chair, day and night. I feel fragmented.

The Necessity of Excretion

This is where the third step comes in: Excretion. To maintain a healthy mental ecosystem, I have to let go. I have to purge the noise to make room for what matters.

My “Excretion Policy” moving forward:

  1. The “No” List: A strict policy on what I will not consume anymore. If it doesn’t add value to my core goals, it’s out.
  2. AI Fasting: Scheduled time where I am completely out of the loop. No models, no prompts, no screens.
  3. Tool Monogamy: I’ve decided to master one tool at a time until I’ve exhausted its utility. Jumping between every new shiny AI tool leads to a satisfied ego but a shallow understanding.
  4. Hand-Coding as Vitality: I am re-learning Python, Rust, and Go through various platforms, not just to generate code, but to understand it deeply enough to write it by hand. It’s about keeping my own cognitive “muscles” alive while the AI does the heavy lifting.

It’s been a fascinating journey so far, but the lesson is clear: If you want to stay sane in the age of AI, you cannot just consume.

Consume. Digest. Excrete. Repeat for system health.
