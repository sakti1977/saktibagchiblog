For the past several months, I have been reading, practicing, and writing about AI — obsessively, almost embarrassingly. And then Claude and OpenAI dropped their latest releases. Claude Cowork arrived. And something shifted.

Earlier, my head was a crowded marketplace of ideas with almost no hands to execute them. Now, the hands are faster than the ideas. That is a strange and wonderful problem to have.

Here is the first sign I noticed something had fundamentally changed in me: I stopped hitting my daily limits on Instagram and YouTube.

For nearly a year, I had set a strict 30-minute cap on both — a small act of sanity in a noisy world. I held that boundary like a promise to myself. But in the past few days, I haven’t even come close to the limit. Not because I am suppressing the urge. Simply because I don’t feel the pull anymore. Practicing, reading, and writing about AI has quietly replaced the scroll.

I got busier. But it’s a different kind of busy.

You know that feeling when you plan a vacation hoping to do absolutely nothing — to just lie on a beach, stare at the sky, let your mind go blank? And then you find yourself hiking at 6 AM, exploring every corner of the city, physically exhausted but oddly alive?

That’s exactly what AI is doing to my calendar.

It was supposed to free up time. And it does — in pockets. But those pockets don’t stay empty for long. They fill up with new experiments, new projects, new questions. Every block of freed time becomes a canvas. The abundance is almost overwhelming.

It feels like a jumbo buffet opening its doors to someone who hasn’t eaten in days. You don’t slow down. You load the plate.

Last week, I turned that energy toward my WordPress website.

I used to spend hours — sometimes entire evenings — editing layouts, customizing pages, wrestling with plugins. It was a labor of love, but labor nonetheless. This time, with AI as my co-pilot, I transformed the pages in minutes.

And then I sat back and asked myself a question that felt slightly absurd given where I was writing it: Do I even need WordPress anymore?

Yes, I realize the irony. Writing a “why I might leave WordPress” post on WordPress. The universe has a sense of humor.

But here is the truth: if I have a domain, I can build pages and host them. It’s not as plug-and-play as WordPress, but with a little discipline and curiosity — both of which seem to be in good supply these days — it is entirely possible. The gatekeeping is gone.

I also built an MCP — a small but satisfying project — that takes a finished blog post and generates shareable content for LinkedIn, Facebook, Twitter, and WhatsApp in one go. My social media posting time is now close to zero.

The tools are falling into place. The workflow is tightening.

But here is what I have realized stays irreplaceable in all of this: original thought.

AI can amplify an idea. It can execute, refine, and distribute it at speed. But the spark — the original observation, the genuine insight, the lived experience — that still has to come from somewhere human. From somewhere inside.

So I am carving out 2.5 hours every day just to read.

Not online articles. Not newsletters. Books. Physical, distraction-free, margin-scribbling books. Because that is where I find the quiet necessary for real absorption. That is where original ideas still have room to breathe.

I am sharing this not as a productivity prescription, but simply as a snapshot of one person’s state of mind in the middle of a strange and exciting moment in history.

If any of this resonates with you — if you too are standing at this same buffet, plate in hand, slightly overwhelmed but smiling — let’s give each other a quiet thumbs up.

We are figuring this out together.
